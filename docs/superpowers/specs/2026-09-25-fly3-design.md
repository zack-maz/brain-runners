# `fly3`, the trained fly: design

Date: 2026-09-25 · Status: the user approved the design section by section in chat (decision 48 of
`docs/DECISIONS.md`; stopped at the probe, decision 49) · Branch `fly3`, cut from `fly2-settle` · Research: `docs/research/2026-09-24-fly/`
(`ecosystem-report.md`, design 1) · Track 2 of decision 41. A fast engine gets its own spec, if the probe asks for one.

## Goal

Answer, with controls, **"does the fly's wiring help a learner play?"**, and only then offer the answer as a player.
`fly3` keeps the brain of `fly` and `fly2` (the untrained FlyWire v783 wiring in the Shiu et al. model, frozen) and
changes two things: the input reaches each looming cell separately, and the move is chosen by a readout learned by
imitating the solver. It is a separate player labelled **trained**. Decision 2 still binds `fly` and `fly2`, which
stay as they are, and fly3 never enters their calibration.

## Why (in short)

- fly2 is the better pure fly (decision 47: 79.45 rows against fly's 66.02 on seeds 1400–1499), but its own no-brain
  control reached 74 of its 82 rows: most of its skill is our mapping and rule.
- fly's and fly2's inputs are sums over channels, so the brain never learns *where* a gap is; spike 03 found that the
  hand-picked output neurons carry "where" only as strength. A readout over every descending neuron (DN) may find
  more.
- The warning from other projects: a flexible trained part can play without the brain (fly-craftax learned "a
  clock"; dino-fly's bare rule beat its brain). The controls below are part of the result, not an extra.
- The brain cannot add information that is not in its input, so "the brain helps" has an honest meaning only for a
  fixed learner: **the same linear readout on the brain's DNs against the same linear readout on the input itself.**
  The question is whether the frozen wiring is a useful layer of features.

## What is the fly's and what is ours

**The fly's:** the wiring and the model (unchanged), the cells that receive input (LPLC2 and LC4 of both eyes), and
the cells that are read (every descending neuron in the FlyWire annotations, about 1,300).

**Ours** (labelled on the page, in `meta.json` and in the write-up):

1. **The per-cell input.** Each LPLC2/LC4 cell has a receptive-field centre on the eye (from the probe, part A). Each
   visible tile (7 lanes × 6 rows ahead in v2) is placed on the eye by its lane offset and distance; a gap drives the
   cells whose field covers it, more strongly the nearer it is (looming). The orientation of the eye's axes is taken
   from published data where it exists; where it does not, it is ours, chosen once before any scoring, and labelled.
2. **The readout.** A softmax over the four moves (stay, left, right, jump) on the DN spike counts of the decision
   window (split into early and late halves if the probe says so), with L1 and L2 penalties and weights starting at
   zero.
3. **The learning.** Imitation of the solver by DAgger (below). The solver sees only what every player sees
   (`bakeoff/players/solver.py`, tiles outside the window count as gaps) and scores 150 on every v2 track, so the
   input holds everything a perfect player needs.
4. **The numbers**, each fixed by a rule written before it is measured: the input strength, the window features, the
   penalties, and whether to average two windows per decision.

**Not in this design:** training the brain itself (weights or neurons), reinforcement learning, a nonlinear readout,
reading neurons other than DNs (the nearer the readout sits to the input, the more it becomes the no-brain control),
carrying the brain's state between rows (20 ms time constants, no adaptation), a new engine, any change to `fly` or
`fly2`.

## Seeds, fixed now

- **Training:** v2 practice seeds 1000–1299 for DAgger; 1300–1399 held out from training to choose the penalties and
  for the probe's play test.
- **Evaluation:** 1400–1499, played once, by fly3 and every control. fly and fly2 have already played these seeds
  (`runs/20260925-163957`); their records are reused, not replayed.
- Nothing below 1000, ever (the tournament's seeds).

## Learning: DAgger on "any safe move"

- **Round 0** follows the solver's own path; every later round plays the current readout (with the solver taking
  over after a death, so the rest of the track is still seen). Every row reached is recorded with its DN counts and
  its labels. The readout is refitted on everything recorded so far. **Five rounds**, 20 practice tracks each (about
  15,000 decisions, about 2 hours at the measured 0.5 s per decision).
- **The target is the set of best moves, not the solver's one move.** `solve_depths` scores every move by how far
  its best continuation reaches; every move that reaches furthest is correct. The fit maximises the probability the
  readout puts on that set (the log of its sum), so a tie among safe moves is never punished. (The probe compares
  this against the single-move target.)
- **Rows that matter weigh more.** A row where some move is fatal (its depth is 0) and another is not gets weight
  `w_critical`; other rows weight 1. `w_critical` is chosen by the probe, from {1, 3, 10}.
- The no-brain control is trained by the same code on the per-cell rates instead of the DN counts; the shuffled
  control by the same code on a shuffled brain.

## Controls (all four, on seeds 1400–1499)

| control | what it rules out | cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **no brain:** the same learner, the same DAgger, fed the per-cell input directly | the input and the learner doing all the work | no simulation; seconds |
| **blind brain:** fly3's readout on a brain whose input is switched off | "a clock": a readout playing from habit, not from sight | one evaluation pass |
| **shuffled wiring:** degree- and sign-preserving shuffle (`bakeoff/fly/shuffle.py`), readout retrained from scratch | any large random network doing as well | a second full training |
| **fly and fly2** | whether training beats the best pure fly | free (already recorded) |

The result is read in this order:
- **"The brain helps"** only if fly3 beats no brain by `bakeoff bench`'s verdict.
- **"The fly's wiring helps"** only if fly3 also beats shuffled wiring. As decision 43 notes, a shuffled brain whose
  DNs barely fire is weak evidence, so the report gives how many DNs fire in each.
- A blind brain close to fly3 means the readout ignores vision, and the result is reported as that.
- The readout's largest weights are reported by DN type, and compared with DNa02, DNa01, DNg13 and the Giant Fiber.

If fly3 does not beat no brain, the write-up says so (the wiring is not needed for this game's skill), and fly3 stays
off the page.

## Order and gates

### 1. The probe (spike 05)

Branch `spike/fly3-probe`, `spikes/05-fly3-probe/REPORT.md`. The code is throwaway: its ideas are ported, not merged.
One real brain, Brian2 as today, about 2–3 hours, free. Every choice rule below is written into the report before
the probe measures anything.

**Part A: a place on the eye for every cell.**
- Start from spike 03: each cell at the synapse-weighted centre of its columnar inputs (T4, T5, Tm5f for LPLC2; T2,
  Tm4, Tm2, Tm3 for LC4).
- Try FlyWire's optic-lobe column map (`flyconnectome/ol_annotations`, Matsliah et al. 2024) for which way the axes
  face: which end is forward, which is up.
- Output: `fields.json`, each cell's centre, its source, and a note on every part that is ours.
- **Stop** if the cells do not spread over the eye (fewer than three distinguishable positions along either axis).

**Part B: does the output know where a gap is?** Single gaps, one tile at a time, in each of the 42 visible tiles,
repeated with different noise seeds; the DN counts recorded per window, split into early and late halves. A readout of
the planned shape is fitted to predict the gap's lane and its row, and scored on repeats it did not see. Measured
here, each by its rule:

1. **Input strength.** Three or four strengths. Keep the one where the DNs predict the lane best; report how many DNs
   fire at each.
2. **Early/late timing.** Keep the split only if it predicts the lane clearly better than one count per window
   (outside the noise between repeats).
3. **Noise averaging.** Measure how much accuracy one window loses against the mean of two. Adopt two windows per
   decision (double the brain time) only if the loss is large (a rule with its threshold fixed in the report first).
- **Pass:** the DNs predict the lane clearly better than chance (1 in 7). Also report the same readout fitted on the
  per-cell rates themselves: part B's first look at no brain.

**Part C: a small play test.** About 1 hour.
- Two or three DAgger rounds on 20 practice tracks (1000–1019). Round 0's recording is fitted both ways, "any safe
  move" and the solver's one move, and with `w_critical` in {1, 3, 10}. Fitting is free, because the features are
  already recorded. Each variant plays 10 tracks from 1300–1399. The best variant carries on.
- Then play fly3, no brain and blind brain on the same 10 tracks.
- **Pass:** fly3 is at least level with no brain, and blind brain plays clearly worse than fly3.
- **Otherwise:** fly3 stops, and the report is the result, as spike 03's was.

**The engine:** the probe reports decisions per second. At 0.5 s a decision the full build is about 8 hours of brain
time (two trainings, three evaluations), which runs overnight on Brian2. A fast engine is proposed only if the
probe's measurement is far worse.

### 2. The build (only if the probe passes)

The build reuses fly2's pieces:

- **`bakeoff/fly/retina.py`** (pure, tested).
  - The cell fields come from the frozen `calibration/fly3_fields.json` (the probe's `fields.json`, with its
    sources).
  - It turns the visible tiles into one rate per cell.
  - Each cell is a channel of one cell for `Brain.window_of`, so `brain.py` does not change how input is driven.
- **`bakeoff/fly/dns.py`** (pure).
  - It selects every descending neuron from the annotations.
  - It turns a window's spikes into one count per DN, or two if split.
  - `Brain` gains that read beside its named readouts. fly's and fly2's readings stay byte-identical, and a parity
    test checks it.
- **`bakeoff/fly/readout.py`** (pure NumPy, no new dependency).
  - The softmax over the four moves, with its set-valued target, row weights, and L1 + L2 penalties.
  - The weights are frozen as `calibration/fly3_readout.json`, together with the rounds, seeds and penalties that
    made them.
  - The penalties are chosen on 1300–1399 by a rule fixed beforehand (the most rows survived, ties to the stronger
    penalty).
- **`bakeoff/fly/train3.py`**: DAgger on 1000–1299, as above.
  - Every decision's DN counts and labels go to disk as they are made, so a crash or Ctrl-C loses at most the
    current track and the run resumes where it stopped.
  - It trains fly3, the no-brain control and the shuffled control with one code path.
- **`bakeoff/players/fly3.py`**: the player. Tiles → per-cell rates → brain → DN counts → frozen readout → move.
  - It logs the four probabilities and the DNs that weighed most in this decision, so the log line can say why.
  - A missing or mismatched `fly3_readout.json` is a usage error before anything is played.
- **The page:**
  - fly3's mind panel: bars for the four move probabilities, the top DNs it leaned on by name, and a permanent
    label "trained: connectome + learned readout (ours)".
  - A row in the lobby's fly block, shown only once the evaluation says fly3 beats no brain. Before that, fly3 is
    reachable from the command line alone.
- **Unchanged:** fly, fly2, their frozen numbers and recorded runs, one brain per process
  (`bakeoff/fly/shared.py`), and one fly process at a time.

### 3. The evaluation

fly3, no brain, blind brain and shuffled wiring play 1400–1499 once. `bakeoff bench` compares them with the fly and
fly2 records of `runs/20260925-163957`. The report is `calibration/FLY3_REPORT.md`, and the result is a decision in
`docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## Testing

- Pure modules (`retina`, `dns`, `readout`, the DAgger bookkeeping) are tested with `uv run pytest`, with no brain and
  no network.
- The readout fit is checked on a synthetic problem with a known answer, including a set-valued target and weighted
  rows.
- A parity test shows that fly's and fly2's readings are unchanged with the DN read added.
- A `-m slow` test plays fly3 for a few rows on the real brain, sharing it with fly and fly2.
- A live smoke test (`bakeoff live` with fly3 ticked) follows every change to the run path, as for fly2: a
  live run plays in a worker thread, where brian2 needs `import_brian2()`.

## Risks

- **The DNs barely fire** (dino-fly: 18 of about 1,300 under looming). The readout then sees mostly zeros and falls
  back on habit. Part B measures it at each input strength; the blind-brain control catches it in play.
- **The eye's orientation is not in published data.** Then it is ours, fixed once, and labelled. A mirrored axis
  would still be learnable, because the readout is learned, but the page must not claim it is the fly's.
- **Noise.** The same row gives different spikes. Part B measures what it costs, and averaging is adopted only by its
  rule.
- **Memory on this 8 GB Mac.** The brain takes about 1 GB, and 15,000 decisions × about 1,300 counts × 2 halves in
  float32 is about 160 MB. Recordings stream to disk and the fit reads them back.
