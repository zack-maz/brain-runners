# `fly2`, the second pure fly: design

Date: 2026-09-24 · Status: the user approved the design section by section in chat (decision 41 of
`docs/DECISIONS.md`) · Branch `fly2`, cut from `phase6-updates` · Research: `docs/research/2026-09-24-fly/` · Track 1
of two; the trained fly (track 2) and a fast engine each get their own spec later.

## Goal

A better fly that is still the pure fly of decision 2: the untrained FlyWire v783 wiring in the Shiu et al. model,
with everything that is ours labelled as ours. `fly2` plays beside `fly`, and the lobby can select either. `fly` stays
as it is: its thresholds, its recorded runs and the spikes it produces.

## Why (the research, in short)

- **The input is the bottleneck.** With `fly`'s input, two eye totals of 11 levels each, the best possible lookup
  table scores 66.7 rows on v2 held-out seeds 1200–1399, and the fly scores 66.0. Re-fixing its thresholds on v2 picks
  the same numbers again (`docs/research/2026-09-24-fly/diagnosis-report.md`).
- **Why it jumps into gaps.** A gap in row 1 in any of the 7 visible lanes drives its eye to the 250 Hz cap, and a gap
  straight ahead drives both eyes. So "gap straight ahead" and "gaps on both sides" give the same input. Only that
  saturated input takes the Giant Fiber past 200 Hz, and row 2, where a jump lands, adds at most one level. All 25
  recorded deaths are jumps into a gap made when a safe move existed.
- **Richer inputs raise the ceiling.** These are inputs of ours, scored as best table on held-out seeds:
  - narrow eyes: 92 rows;
  - a straight-ahead channel: 101 rows;
  - narrow eyes split near/far: 109 rows;
  - a 3×3 patch: 136 rows.
- **The wiring** (`circuits-report.md`):
  - DNb01 has no reported walking-steering role. DNa02 is the established walking-steering descending neuron, with
    DNa01 and DNg13.
  - LPLC2 and LC4 mainly drive the takeoff descending neurons.
  - No visual projection type can veto a jump.
  - LPLC4 and LC22 are a sideways route that barely touches the Giant Fiber, but its turn direction is not known.
- **The warning from other projects** (`ecosystem-report.md`): our mapping and our rule can do the work while the
  brain adds nothing (dino-fly: brain 83, bare threshold 281). The controls below are part of the result.

## What is the fly's and what is ours

**The fly's:** the wiring and the model (unchanged), which cells receive input, and which cells are read.

**Ours** (labelled on the page, in `meta.json` and in the write-up):

1. **The input mapping.** One of three candidates wins by the rule below.

   | candidate | channels | a gap in the runner's lane (row r) | a gap in a side lane (row r) |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | M1, a straight-ahead channel | `centre`, `left`, `right` | `centre`: LPLC2 on both sides | offsets −3 to −1: `left` = LC4 of the left eye; offsets 1 to 3: `right` = LC4 of the right eye |
   | M2, narrow eyes | `left`, `right` | both eyes (LPLC2 + LC4 of each), as `fly` does | offset −1 only: `left`; offset 1 only: `right`; farther lanes are not shown |
   | M3, a sideways channel | `centre`, `left`, `right` | `centre`: LPLC2 + LC4 on both sides | offsets −3 to −1: `left` = LPLC4 + LC22 of the left eye; offsets 1 to 3: the same on the right |

   Each channel's rate is Σ `gain / r ** falloff` over its gaps, capped at 500 Hz. It is quantized to that
   candidate's levels: 6 levels (0 to 500 Hz, 100 Hz steps) for three-channel candidates and 11 levels (0 to 500 Hz,
   50 Hz steps) for M2, so every input a candidate can produce is on its measured surface. Lifting the cap is what
   lets "centre gap plus one side gap" differ from "centre gap alone".
2. **The readout.**
   - **Turn** = (DNa02 + DNa01 + DNg13) on the right minus the same on the left, by soma side. Each of these turns
     the fly toward its own side (Rayshubskiy 2025, Yang 2024), so a positive turn means right. DNb01 is left out.
     The probe can remove DNa02 (see "Order and gates").
   - **Jump** = the mean of the Giant Fiber (DNp01), as for `fly`.
3. **The rule: dodge before jump.** If |turn| is above the turn threshold, go right or left. Otherwise, if jump is
   above the jump threshold, jump. Otherwise stay. (`fly` lets the jump win.)
4. **The numbers:** gain, falloff and the two thresholds, fixed on practice seeds by the rule below and then frozen.

M3 is admitted only if the probe shows that driving one eye's LPLC4 + LC22 turns the fly away from that side. If it
turns toward the gap, reading it as "away" would be our relabelling, which decision 2 forbids, so M3 is dropped.

**Not in this update:**
- memory between decisions (the model has 20 ms time constants and no adaptation, so it would buy little);
- the landing-row glance before a jump (mostly ours);
- the fast engine;
- the trained fly;
- any change to `fly`.

## Order and gates

1. **The probe** (spike 04, branch `spike/fly2-probe`, `spikes/04-fly2-probe/REPORT.md`; throwaway code, one real
   brain, about 20 minutes). It uses 100 ms windows, 8 seeded trials per condition, and reads DNa01, DNa02, DNg13,
   DNb01, DNb05, DNa04 and DNp01 on both sides.
   - **One-sided and uneven drive.** Each candidate channel is driven alone at 100, 300 and 500 Hz. Then a strong
     `centre` is combined with an uneven `left`/`right`: `centre` 500 Hz with `left` 0, 100, 300 or 500 Hz and `right`
     0 Hz, and the mirror image. This answers whether DNa02, DNa01 and DNg13 keep a left/right difference that points
     away from the gap when the centre is driven hard.
   - **DNa02 cancels.** If DNa02's right-minus-left is within the trial noise (below one standard deviation of the
     trials) in the uneven-with-strong-centre conditions, DNa02 leaves the turn sum. The turn is then DNa01 + DNg13.
   - **M3's direction.** If `left` (LPLC4 + LC22) alone gives a turn toward the left, M3 is dropped.
   - **The gate.** If no candidate's turn points away from the gap side in the uneven-with-strong-centre conditions,
     the update stops, as update 2b did, and the report is the result.
   - **Outcome (2026-09-24, decision 42):** the gate passed and M3 is admitted. All three candidates go on to
     step 2. The turn readout is per candidate: M2 is DNa01 + DNg13, while M1 and M3 keep DNa02 + DNa01 + DNg13.
2. **Surfaces.** One response surface per surviving candidate on the real brain: every combination of its channel
   levels, 8 trials each. That is 216 cells for M1 and M3 (about 20–30 minutes each) and 121 cells for M2 (about 15
   minutes), written to `calibration/fly2_surface_<candidate>.json`.
3. **The rule, fixed before any surface exists** (written in the plan's first task, as code and docstring):
   - For each candidate, a grid over gain (100, 250, 500 Hz), falloff (2, 3, 4), turn threshold (0, 10, 20, 40 Hz)
     and jump threshold (100 to 300 Hz in 25 Hz steps).
   - Every grid point plays v2 practice seeds 1000–1199 with the stand-in brain made from that candidate's surface.
   - The highest mean rows wins. Ties go to candidates in the order M1, M2, M3, then to grid order.
   - The winner plays held-out seeds 1200–1399 once.
   - Written to `calibration/FLY2_REPORT.md`. The constants are then frozen in `bakeoff/players/fly2.py`. Nothing
     below 1000 is ever played.
4. **The controls,** on the same seeds, in `calibration/FLY2_REPORT.md`:
   - **No brain:** the winning candidate's channel rates put through the same rule with the brain skipped.
     - Turn signal = `left` − `right` (a gap on the left turns right).
     - Jump signal = the sum of all channels.
     - Its own grid: the winner's gain and falloff grid, turn threshold 0, 50, 100 or 200 Hz, and jump threshold
       100 to 1,500 Hz in 100 Hz steps.
     - It is scored on the same seeds by the same rule.
   - **Shuffled wiring:** one surface of the winning candidate on the wiring shuffled with a fixed seed, keeping
     every neuron's in-degree, out-degree and every synapse's sign. It is played by the same rule with its own
     thresholds.
   - **`fly`** itself.

   How to read them: if the no-brain control plays as well as `fly2`, the write-up says the gain came from our
   mapping and rule, not from the wiring.
5. **The player and the page** (below).
6. **The real brain** plays v2 practice seeds 1000–1019 once, `fly` and `fly2` in one run. It is recorded in
   `docs/COSTS.md` and benchmarked with `bakeoff bench`.

## Code

- **`bakeoff/fly/channels.py` (new, pure).**
  - A `Mapping` per candidate: its channel names, each channel's cell types and sides, and its levels.
  - `rates(senses, mapping, gain, falloff) -> dict[channel, hz]`, already quantized.
  - `looming_rates` in `bakeoff/senses.py` stays exactly as it is for `fly`.
- **`bakeoff/fly/neurons.py`.**
  - Input cells are selected per channel, from a map of channel to cell types and sides. `fly`'s channels are
    `left`/`right` = LPLC2 + LC4 of that side, and select exactly today's cells.
  - Read-out types gain DNg13, DNb05 and DNa04. The one-neuron-per-group guard of the surface holds: each has one
    cell per side.
- **`bakeoff/fly/brain.py`: one brain, one input group per player.**
  - The model is built once, and each registered input (a named selection of channels) gets its own `PoissonGroup`
    and wiring.
  - `window(input_name, rates_by_channel, noise_seed)` turns on only that input's group; every other group gets
    `active = False`, so it runs no code and draws no random numbers.
  - A fly-only run registers only `fly`'s input, so it is the network of today.
  - **Parity gate (slow test):** with both inputs registered, `fly` replays its recorded v2 runs
    (`runs/20260921-192037`, seeds 1000–1004) with identical spike counts at every decision. If that fails, the
    work stops and the user decides; drift is never accepted silently.
  - `shuffle_seed` (for the control only) swaps the model's connections while keeping degrees and signs.
- **`bakeoff/players/fly2.py`.**
  - The winning mapping, the readout, the rule and the frozen constants (`CALIBRATED = False` until step 3 ends; the
    CLI refuses to play an uncalibrated `fly2` outside calibration).
  - Its `info` logs every channel's rate, every read-out's rate and spike times, turn and jump signals, and which
    branch of the rule fired.
  - It shares the process's brain with `fly`: the first fly to reset builds it, and the others register their inputs
    on it. The brain still needs the input registered before `store("clean")`, so all fly players in a run register
    before the first window.
- **`bakeoff/fly/surface.py`.**
  - Schema 2 has any number of named channels: `{"channels": [...], "levels_hz": {...}, "cells": [...]}`.
  - Schema 1 still loads, so `fly`'s stand-in is unchanged.
  - `measure_surface` takes a mapping.
- **`bakeoff/fly/calibrate.py`.**
  - `search` takes a player factory and a grid, so `fly`'s own calibration still reproduces
    `calibration/REPORT.md`.
  - A new entry point runs step 3 and writes `calibration/FLY2_REPORT.md`.
- **Plumbing:**
  - `fly2` is registered in `bakeoff/players/__init__.py` and in `CONTESTANTS` of `bakeoff/replay.py`.
  - Its frozen constants go into `meta.json` as `fly`'s do (`bakeoff/runner.py`).
  - The lobby offers it (free player).

## The page

- **The lobby:** the fly row has two ticks, each saying what it is.
  - `fly`: "looming → escape reflex (phase 2)".
  - `fly2`: the winning candidate's one-line summary, e.g. "straight-ahead channel, walking-steering neurons, dodge
    before jump".
- **The tunnel:** `fly2` uses the fly sprite, told apart by its label (blue stays the cursor only).
- **The mind panel** (`viewer/minds.js`): each channel's input rate, the turn from its three neurons against its
  threshold, the Giant Fiber against its threshold, and which branch of the rule fired.
- **The log line** (`viewer/log.js`) says the input is ours, as `fly`'s does.
- **"What is ours"** lists the mapping, the readout set, the rule, the numbers, and the three controls' scores.
- All text from a log is escaped (`Minds.esc`), and nothing loads from the network.

## Tests

- **Fast** (no brain built, `uv run pytest`):
  - each candidate's `rates` from hand-written senses, including the cap and the quantization;
  - the rule's order: dodge before jump, and ties;
  - channel selection gives `fly` exactly today's cells;
  - surface schemas 1 and 2 both load, and a missing input level is an error that names it;
  - the calibration rule's winner and tie-breaking on a small fake surface;
  - `fly2` registered and refused while uncalibrated;
  - `meta.json` carries its constants;
  - the page's pure JavaScript: `minds.js`, `log.js` and `lobby.js` for `fly2`.
- **Slow** (`uv run pytest -m slow`, one brain at a time):
  - the parity gate above;
  - `fly2` plays one v2 practice track end to end;
  - a live run with `fly` and `fly2` from the lobby, as the notes on live runs require after any change to the run
    path.

## Risks

- **DNa02 cancelling out.** When both eyes are driven hard, DNa02 has been seen to cancel. The probe decides, and
  DNa01 + DNg13 is the fall-back.
- **A new death mode.** Dodging into a side-lane gap in row 1 is now possible. Every candidate's channels show row-1
  side gaps, so this should be rare. The report counts deaths by kind.
- **Parity.** The shared brain might change `fly`'s spikes. The slow parity test is the gate, and the user decides if
  it fails.
- **Our share of the gain.** The rule and the mapping may do the work. The controls measure this, and the write-up
  reports them whatever they show.
- **Memory.** One brain is about 1 GB. Two fly players share it, and only one fly process ever runs.
